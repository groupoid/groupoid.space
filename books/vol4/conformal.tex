\begin{abstract}
We package Philip~D.\ Mannheim's conformal gravity (Weyl-squared gravity) into the canonical article structure of the Groupoid Infinity Institute. The theory is locally conformal invariant, power-counting renormalizable, and unitary via \(PT\)-symmetry. All curvature arises from quantization; the zero-point energy of gravity identically cancels that of matter, and spontaneous breaking of conformal symmetry cancels the induced cosmological constant. Macroscopically the theory accounts for galactic rotation curves without dark matter. We record the exact static spherically-symmetric solution, its de~Sitter embedding, the conformal-to-flat de~Sitter metric, and the global quadratic potential induced by cosmic inhomogeneities. Classical Einstein and Maxwell equations appear as infrared instances. Distinct quantizations (string/M-theory in the Urs Schreiber homotopy-super-smooth type system, and Loop Quantum Gravity) emerge as bifurcations of the conformal parent theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Einstein gravity is sufficient but not necessary for the observed solar-system geometry. Any viable metric theory must recover the Schwarzschild solution (to the tested order) while remaining generally covariant. Conformal gravity realises this requirement by elevating local conformal invariance
\[
g_{\mu\nu}(x)\;\mapsto\;\Omega^2(x)\,g_{\mu\nu}(x)
\]
to a fundamental symmetry. The unique local action built from the Weyl tensor yields a fourth-order theory that is renormalizable, free of a priori classical curvature, and capable of cancelling both the vacuum energy and the cosmological constant.

The present note packages Mannheim's framework \cite{mannheim2011,mannheim1990} into the Groupoid Infinity canonical style, records the relevant de~Sitter formulae, and indicates how string/M-theory (Urs Schreiber) and Loop Quantum Gravity arise as distinct bifurcations of the same conformal parent.

\section{Conformal Gravity Action and Field Equations}
\label{sec:action}

\subsection{Weyl Tensor and Bach Equation}

The Weyl conformal tensor is the traceless, conformally invariant part of the Riemann tensor:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}
&= R_{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}
+\tfrac16 R\bigl(g_{\lambda\nu}g_{\mu\kappa}-g_{\lambda\kappa}g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)\\
&\quad-\tfrac12\bigl(g_{\lambda\nu}R_{\mu\kappa}-g_{\lambda\kappa}R_{\mu\nu}
-g_{\mu\nu}R_{\lambda\kappa}+g_{\mu\kappa}R_{\lambda\nu}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The unique (up to total derivatives) local, generally covariant and locally conformal invariant action in four dimensions is
\begin{equation}
I_W=-\alpha_g\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,C_{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}C^{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}
=-2\alpha_g\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\Bigl(R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}-\tfrac13 R^2\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Variation with respect to the metric produces the Bach equation
\begin{equation}
-4\alpha_g\,W^{\mu\nu}+T_M^{\mu\nu}=0,
\end{equation}
where the Bach tensor \(W^{\mu\nu}\) is fourth-order, traceless and divergenceless:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
W^{\mu\nu}
&=\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}(R)^{;\beta}{}_{;\beta}+R^{\mu\nu;\beta}{}_{;\beta}
-R^{\mu\beta;\nu}{}_{;\beta}-R^{\nu\beta;\mu}{}_{;\beta}\\
&\quad-2R^{\mu\beta}R^\nu{}_\beta+\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}R_{\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}
-\tfrac23 g^{\mu\nu}(R)^{;\beta}{}_{;\beta}\\
&\quad+\tfrac23(R)^{;\mu;\nu}+\tfrac23 R\,R^{\mu\nu}-\tfrac16 g^{\mu\nu}R^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Every Ricci-flat metric (hence every vacuum solution of Einstein gravity) solves the Bach equation. The converse is false: conformal gravity admits a larger solution space.

\section{Exact Static Spherically-Symmetric Solution}
\label{sec:exact}

Outside a static, spherically-symmetric source the line element may be written
\begin{equation}
ds^2=-B(r)\,dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{B(r)}+r^2\,d\Omega^2.
\end{equation}
The exact solution of the Bach equation is
\begin{equation}
B(r)=1-\frac{\beta(2-3\beta\gamma)}{r}-3\beta\gamma+\gamma r-\kappa r^2,
\end{equation}
where \(\beta\) is related to the Newtonian mass, \(\gamma\) generates the linear potential responsible for galactic rotation curves, and \(\kappa\) embeds the solution in a de~Sitter-like cosmology. The linear term \(\gamma r\) accounts for the observed rotation curves of 138 galaxies with no adjustable parameters other than the stellar mass-to-light ratio and with no dark-matter halo.

For weak gravity the circular velocity receives the asymptotic contribution
\begin{equation}
\frac{v^2}{R}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{N^*\beta^*c^2}{R^2}+\frac{N^*\gamma^*c^2}{2}+\frac{\gamma_0 c^2}{2}-\kappa c^2 R.
\end{equation}
The global quadratic term \(-\kappa c^2 R\) is induced by inhomogeneities in the cosmic background and is observationally measured at \(\kappa\simeq9.54\times10^{-54}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-2}\).

\section{de~Sitter Geometry in Conformal Gravity}
\label{sec:desitter}

\subsection{Conformal-to-Flat de~Sitter Metric}

Any geometry conformal to Minkowski space has vanishing Weyl tensor and therefore solves the vacuum Bach equation. The cosmologically relevant de~Sitter and Robertson--Walker metrics belong to this class:
\begin{equation}
ds^2=\Omega^2(t,x,y,z)\bigl[-dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2\bigr].
\end{equation}
An explicit de~Sitter factor is
\begin{equation}
\Omega(t)=\frac{1}{H\cosh(Ht)}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\Omega(t)=e^{Ht}
\end{equation}
(in appropriate coordinates), yielding constant positive curvature
\begin{equation}
R_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu},\qquad\Lambda=3H^2.
\end{equation}
Thus conformal gravity admits a pure de~Sitter solution with no cosmological-constant term present in the fundamental action; the effective \(\Lambda\) is generated geometrically by the conformal factor.

\subsection{Global Quadratic Potential and Cosmic Embedding}

The term \(-\kappa r^2\) that appears in the exact exterior solution is de~Sitter-like. It is not associated with a maximally symmetric geometry (the source of \(\kappa\) is the inhomogeneous cosmic background), yet a test particle orbiting inside a galaxy feels the same quadratic potential that would arise from a pure de~Sitter embedding. In this sense every galactic rotation curve measures a global de~Sitter radius induced by the rest of the universe.

\subsection{Perturbations about Conformal de~Sitter}

First-order metric fluctuations about a conformal-to-flat de~Sitter background obey a linearised Bach equation that can be mapped, by a further conformal transformation, onto fluctuations about flat space. Tensor modes (gravitational waves) therefore inherit the same conformal structure, even though the perturbed geometry itself need not remain conformal to flat.

\section{Classical Einstein and Maxwell Equations as Instances}

\begin{itemize}

\item Maxwell theory is already conformally invariant in four dimensions. Its stress tensor is traceless and couples consistently to the Bach tensor. Both the Einstein and Maxwell equations therefore appear as particular solutions (or infrared limits) of the single Bach equation coupled to a conformally coupled matter sector.
\end{itemize}

\label{sec:conclusion}

Classical Einstein and Maxwell theories sit inside the conformal parent as infrared instances.
